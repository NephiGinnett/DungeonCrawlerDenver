# Story Premise — Denver, Foreclosed

**Status: draft v2, 2026-08-23.** Supersedes v1 in place — v1 treated Denver's repossession
premise as the whole story; this version keeps everything from v1 as Denver's *local flavor*
and puts a planet-scale conspiracy underneath it, per Zoe's own steer. Living document: edit
in place as the story develops, same as `floor-01-design.md`. Do not create a dated
"correction" file next to this one.

---

## Why this document exists

Dungeon Crawler Carl's real engine isn't "evil AI" or "monsters." It's corporate
colonization executed with the affect of a quarterly earnings call — and, per actual DCC
canon (not invented for this document), the whole premise already runs on a dual motive: the
Dungeon is *presented* to survivors as a way to reclaim Earth's resources from the aliens who
took it, while *actually* being a galactic gameshow broadcast for entertainment revenue, AND
the legal mechanism the aliens use to strip-mine the planet outright. Two real motives hiding
behind one cover story. This document's whole job is pointing this project's own systems
(System AI, AI Favor, the repossession flavor, the floor structure itself) at that same
dual-motive shape, then adding the two things canon doesn't hand us for free: named Earth-side
collaborators, and a concrete "what are they actually digging for."

---

## The real premise (planet-scale, not Denver-scale)

**Earth defaulted a long time before anyone alive remembers agreeing to anything.**

Per Dungeon Crawler Carl's own canon: any species gets 50 years after First Contact to file a
proper legal claim to their own planet's resources. Default it, and the claiming party gets
the planet outright — no rights, no appeal, no requirement to even ask first. Earth's First
Contact happened during Ancient Egypt. Whatever claim should have been filed has been overdue
for thousands of years. What looks, from ground level, like a sudden invasion is really a
foreclosure notice arriving several millennia late, executed all at once, planet-wide, in a
single event survivors call **the Collapse** — every roofed structure gone in the same
instant, everywhere on Earth, no warning.

**The Borant Corporation** — a Kua-Tin megacorporation, nominally a mining concern, whose
*actual* revenue is broadcast, advertising, licensing, and sponsor-commission money — is the
Syndicate-appointed planetary regent of Earth as a result. It produces the show survivors are
now living inside: **Dungeon Crawler World: Earth.** The cover story it tells survivors
(descend, loot, and you can theoretically buy the planet back) is real enough that people
attempt it. It is not the real business model.

**The real business model is two things happening at once, using the same raw material:**

1. **Entertainment.** Quintillions of viewers across the Syndicate's three billion star
   systems watch crawlers die for sport. AI Favor is this half of the economy made playable —
   every point of Favor a crawler earns is a real transaction, audience attention converted
   into a resource that keeps a specific crawler alive a little longer. It is also, quietly,
   a tally of how much of a given life has already been sold.
2. **Extraction.** The Dungeon's floors are not set-dressing over an existing city. They are
   excavation shafts — the actual, physical, "journey to the center of the Earth" kind. Every
   floor descended is real depth mined, and what's being mined is not only ore. Crawler
   deaths, recorded and metabolized somewhere beneath the lowest floor anyone has ever
   reached, are themselves a harvested resource — the literal meaning of "spending human lives
   as currency," not a metaphor this document is being cute about. **Nobody in the party
   knows this yet, and neither does the audience.** Both halves of the economy are being told
   a different, more palatable story than the one actually running.

**Denver is one regional entry point into this single, planet-wide structure** — not its own
separate dungeon. New York, Tokyo, and every other surviving population center have their own
entry points, their own local flavor, and (as far as anyone in Denver currently knows) their
own crawls running in parallel. All of them, per the excavation-shaft idea above, are
plausibly converging on the same target as they go deeper. Nothing says the party ever meets
another city's crawlers — but nothing rules it out later, either.

**The four million-odd crawlers this project's Population subsystem already tracks are
volunteers specifically** — survivors of the Collapse who chose to formally enter the
competitive Dungeon product, as opposed to whatever thinner, harder, unbroadcast life is left
for everyone who didn't. That distinction — volunteer vs. survivor — is worth surfacing in
narration occasionally. It is the difference between "this happened to me" and "I signed up
for this," and DCC's own comedy lives in how thin that distinction actually is once you look
at what the alternative was.

## Denver's local flavor is still exactly what it was (nothing here is wasted)

Everything already built about repossession, foreclosure, Voldemortgage, and the Debt Eaters
is **Denver's specific dramatization of the real, planet-wide legal situation** — Borant's
writers picked a theme per city to keep the show varied, and Denver got "you owe a debt, this
is collections" because it's an unusually on-the-nose way to dramatize what actually happened
to the whole species. Voldemortgage is not lying, exactly. He is just a floor boss doing a
bit about something that is, one legal layer up, literally true. Nothing about Floor 1 needs
to change. It just now rhymes with the real premise instead of floating independently of it.

`Mile High Recovery Solutions`, proposed in v1 as the top-level villain, is demoted to
**Denver's specific in-fiction production shell** — a Borant-licensed local studio, the
"network affiliate" running this city's segment of Dungeon Crawler World and dressing it in
debt-collection drag. It can still show up as a named, on-screen entity (letterhead, an
announcement credit, a sponsor bumper) without carrying the weight of being the actual
villain, because it isn't one — it's a production company with a theme, several rungs below
the people who actually decided Earth was worth taking.

## Earth's own worst actors signed on (the human collaborators)

The satirical core of this revision: **real, present-day Earth corporations partnered with
the aliens almost immediately**, not because they were forced to, but because they saw a
once-in-a-species opportunity to buy a seat at a table three billion star systems wide before
anyone else on Earth even understood the table existed. They are wildly outclassed —
technologically, informationally, in every way that matters — and are betting that being
*early* matters more than being *equipped*. It's the exact same instinct that drives a real
tech company to burn money establishing market position before profitability, aimed at a
market that could annihilate them by accident. That gap between their confidence and their
actual leverage is the joke, and it should stay funny rather than turning them into a
competent secondary threat.

Proposed pairings — the first two are Zoe's own, kept as given; the rest are proposals to
keep, swap, or cut:

| Alien side | What it actually is (real DCC canon) | Earth partner | Why the pairing works |
|---|---|---|
| **The Syndicate** | The galactic governing bureaucracy itself — courts, councils, subcommittees, the body whose 50-year-claim rule is the actual reason Earth was forfeit | **Apple** *(Zoe's pairing)* | A walled garden with its own review board deciding what's "allowed to exist" is already a miniature Syndicate. Buying a seat in the real one is just Apple doing the App Store at civilizational scale. |
| **Borant Corporation** | Kua-Tin megacorp, Earth's planetary regent, produces the show, real revenue is broadcast/ads/licensing behind a mining-concern cover | **Microsoft** *(Zoe's pairing)* | Cloud infrastructure as the actual backbone under a consumer-facing product, an entertainment arm (Xbox) riding along, and a running bet on AI dominance — all already true of Microsoft before any aliens showed up. |
| **Valtay Corporation** | Brain-worm parasites (Gondii) that pilot corpses as vehicles; manufactures portals, weapons, personal shields, neural enhancers. *(This is already DCC's own Goa'uld — same shape, no import needed; leaning into a System-Lords-style internal rivalry and "posing as something greater than the corpse it's wearing" plays that up on purpose.)* | **Palantir** *(proposed)* | A company whose actual business is embedding itself inside institutions and operating through them, wearing the host's face in public while making the real decisions, is uncomfortably close to a brain-worm-piloting-a-corpse as a business model already. |
| **Titan Conglomerate** | Named in canon; heavy-industry-coded, minimal detail available beyond the name | **ExxonMobil** *(proposed)* | The one pairing that needs no cover story. "Keep extracting from a dying host past the point of any reasonable person stopping" is already the whole business. |

**One original addition, not from DCC canon** — flagged clearly as original rather than
imported, matching how this project already handles Voldemortgage (inspired by the books'
voice, none of the actual content reused):

**The Undermind.** A hive intelligence, StarCraft-Zerg-flavored in spirit but original in
name and detail, that does the *literal* digging. Wordless, biological, consuming — the thing
actually operating the excavation shafts the floors metaphorically (and, per the premise
above, literally) are. Nobody has contracted with the Undermind. Nobody negotiates with it.
It is closer to a force of nature Borant is *using* than a corporation Borant is partnered
with, and that distinction matters: the corporate floors argue and posture and send strongly
worded memos; whatever the party eventually finds in the deepest, oldest floors should not
talk at all. This is the natural home for this project's escalation from corporate-satire
horror (bureaucratic, verbal, administrative) into something with no bureaucracy left in it -
a floor-depth dial the party can feel, not just a difficulty number going up.

## The party's throughline (rough, intentionally not floor-by-floor)

Unchanged in spirit from v1, now with more to actually discover:

- **Floors 1–2 (Tutorial Floors):** Denver's local flavor plays completely straight.
  Voldemortgage reads as a boss with a theme, not yet as a clue.
- **Early-mid floors:** the first crack — something that doesn't fit the "reclaim the
  planet" cover story. A sponsor logo that shouldn't exist yet. A production credit for
  a company the party recognizes. The first hint that "floor" and "depth" are not
  metaphors.
- **Mid floors:** the Earth-corp collaborators start becoming visible as themselves, not
  just as an alien production's sponsors — the party starts to understand that some of what's
  hunting them, gating them, or "helping" them for a price is *human*, and chose this.
- **Deep floors (unplanned, deliberately):** the corporate satire thins out and the
  Undermind's register takes over. This is where "journey to the center of the Earth" pays
  off as a felt experience, not just a line in a design doc. What's actually down there is
  intentionally unscoped — build floors that could support several answers, the same
  guidance as v1.

AI Favor should keep being played as occasionally uncomfortable rather than purely a reward
resource — now with a concrete reason why: it's not just "the audience wants a show," it's a
running receipt for a transaction nobody consented to.

## What's explicitly NOT decided here

- Whether the party ever learns the *exact* amount being extracted, or whether "we don't
  know how bad it really is" stays permanent.
- Whether other cities' crawls are ever actually shown, or stay a rumor.
- The corp-pairing table above — Palantir/ExxonMobil are proposals, Apple/Microsoft are
  Zoe's own and should stay unless she says otherwise.
- Whether the Undermind is ever named on-screen in-fiction, or stays something the party
  never gets a word for.
- Any actual endgame. Still open, same as v1.
