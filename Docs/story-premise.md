# Story Premise — Denver, Foreclosed

**Status: draft v1, 2026-08-23.** This is the gap-filler CLAUDE.md's design doc list didn't
have — `dcc-bg3-design-doc.md` covers systems, `dungeon-tone-bible.md` covers voice, and
nothing until now stated the actual theme and throughline in one place. Treat this as a
living document: update it in place as the story develops, the same way `floor-01-design.md`
tracks the floor. Do not create a second "corrected" version next to it — edit this one.

---

## Why this document exists

Dungeon Crawler Carl's real engine isn't "evil AI" or "monsters." It's corporate
colonization executed with the affect of a quarterly earnings call — the Syndicate/Borant
Corporation isn't a mustache-twirling villain, it's *worse*: indifferent, incentive-driven,
and completely sincere about the fact that your death is a deliverable. Carl and Donut don't
just endure this. They fight it, exploit its loopholes, and route around it — and the found
family the crawl assembles along the way is the one thing the system can't monetize into
submission.

Everything in this game's tone bible already nails the *voice* of that same idea (the System
AI as "a producer with a quota," never a villain, always disappointed by engagement numbers).
What was missing was naming the shape underneath it — who's actually doing this, why Denver,
and what the party is working toward beyond "clear the next floor."

---

## The core theme

**Denver didn't fall. It got repossessed.**

Not metaphorically — the tone bible already says this about individual buildings ("the
dungeon didn't build these places, it repossessed them"). This document elevates that from a
per-room joke to the actual premise: the whole city defaulted on a debt nobody remembers
agreeing to, and something with the power to do it foreclosed on all four million residents
at once, converting them into "crawlers" — contestants in a broadcast product whose suffering
is the interest payment.

Four throughlines, all already partially built, now named on purpose:

1. **AI prevalence, played as banality rather than menace.** The System AI is never a HAL-9000
   villain. It is a customer service voice that has automated away the need for anyone to feel
   bad about what's happening. This is the "our AI overlords" fear made literal — not that the
   machine hates you, but that it was never built to notice you as anything but a metric.
2. **Poverty and the imbalance of power, made mechanical.** The Population subsystem's "4.18
   million crawlers, you will never meet almost all of them" is this theme as a number. AI
   Favor is this theme as a resource: your survival is measurably better when you're
   *entertaining*, which is a tax specifically on people with the least power in the
   relationship.
3. **Corporations as the real monster, executed as bureaucracy, not horror.** Voldemortgage,
   Debt Eaters, "Unclaimed Property," repossession paperwork. The floor bosses are middle
   management. Nothing evil here raises its voice.
4. **The strength of people joining together, as the one resource the system can't repossess.**
   Fist-bumps, the escort/follow mechanics, first aid, revives — mechanically, the party
   helping each other is *already* the game's actual counter-economy to the dungeon's
   extraction economy. This document just says so out loud, so future content leans into it
   rather than treating it as incidental.

## Who's doing this (draft — Zoe's call, not locked)

No umbrella entity above Voldemortgage exists yet in any doc. Proposing one rather than
leaving the "who" implicit forever:

**Working name: Mile High Recovery Solutions.** A repossession-and-broadcast conglomerate,
playing the same institutional role Borant/the Syndicate play in the books — it owns the
"Dungeon" product, broadcasts it, and profits from both the suffering and the merchandising.
Chosen for the same reason "Voldemortgage" works: it reads exactly like a real debt-collection
agency's actual name, which is the joke. Denver was the collateral; the crawl is the
collection process; the System AI is Mile High's customer-facing product, not a separate
character.

**Open question, deliberately not answered yet:** *why* Denver defaulted. A few directions,
none chosen:
- A literal, cosmic-scale debt — the city (or the state, or the country) borrowed something
  real from something real, and stopped paying.
- A fabricated default — the debt was engineered or falsified, and part of the party's arc is
  discovering this, which reframes "escape the dungeon" into "expose the fraud."
- Something more personal — the crawlers themselves (or their families) are specifically who
  defaulted, not the city abstractly, which would make this a lot more pointed and would need
  checking against how comfortable the table wants this to get.

The fabricated-default direction is the closest match to the books' own arc (Carl's actual
throughline is exposing and fighting the Syndicate, not just surviving it) and is the
recommendation here, but this is exactly the kind of call that should come from the table,
not from a design doc.

## The party's throughline (rough, intentionally not floor-by-floor)

Five ordinary Denver people, transformed into crawlers through no fault of their own, forced
through floors individually rigged against them — but where the one resource Mile High cannot
repossess is each other. The tabletop game's real subject was never the dungeon; it's what
five specific people become to each other under that much pressure. Mechanically, this game
already agrees: escort AI, fist-bump friendship, first aid, revives, shared loot, an ally who
gears up on their own initiative to keep the group alive.

**Rough arc shape**, matching the books' own escalation without copying their specifics:

- **Floors 1–2 (Tutorial Floors, per the real ruleset):** survival as the only visible goal.
  The party doesn't yet know there's anything to fight except the floor in front of them.
  Floor 1 (Cliff Diver Cantina) already does this well — Voldemortgage reads as a boss, not
  yet as a symptom of something larger.
- **Somewhere in the early-mid floors:** the first real crack in "this is just a dungeon" —
  evidence that the debt, the default, or the whole premise doesn't add up. This is where the
  fabricated-default thread (if chosen) would first surface.
- **Later floors (unplanned, deliberately):** the throughline shifts from *surviving* Mile
  High's product to *undermining* it — matching Carl's own arc from contestant to active
  threat to the Syndicate. What that looks like mechanically (sabotage? audience manipulation
  via AI Favor, turned into a weapon instead of just a survival resource? something else
  entirely?) is unscoped on purpose. Don't build toward a specific ending yet; build floors
  that could support several.

## What this means for content going forward

- **AI Favor should occasionally be played as uncomfortable, not just as a resource.** A
  beat where the audience wants something cruel, and getting Favor for it costs something
  narratively, is on-theme and currently unbuilt.
- **Floor bosses should keep being bureaucrats, not monsters.** Voldemortgage and the
  Oathgate's Arbiter already do this right. Keep doing it.
- **Ally/party mechanics (first aid, revives, fist-bumps, escort) are thematically load-bearing,
  not just convenience features.** Worth calling out in dialogue/narration occasionally, not
  just letting them be silent systems.
- **Nothing here requires an alien Syndicate.** Deliberately keeping Mile High human/corporate
  rather than extraterrestrial — it's a sharper, more specific joke for a Denver-set game, and
  avoids reproducing the books' own antagonist shape wholesale.

## What's explicitly NOT decided here

- The fraud-vs-real-debt question above.
- Whether Mile High Recovery Solutions is a good permanent name or a placeholder.
- What floor (if any) the "crack in the premise" beat belongs on.
- Whether there's a defined endgame at all, or whether this stays open-ended like a long
  tabletop campaign.

These are the next things to nail down, in roughly that order.
