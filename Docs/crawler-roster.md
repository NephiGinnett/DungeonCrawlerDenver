# The Named Crawlers — Denver roster

33 authored crawlers. **Generated from `Tools/gen_crawler_roster.py` — edit that file, not this one.** This document and `Docs/DT_DCCCrawlers.csv` are emitted from the same list, so the prose and the table cannot drift apart.

## How this fits the population tiers

`Docs/population-design.md` sets a budget of **around twelve** Tier 1 characters for the whole game, and warns that *“a procedural name with a random stat block is somebody nobody can care about.”* Both still hold. Nothing here contradicts them, because **being named is not the same as being built.**

The doc's real concern is cost: fifty half-simulated characters cost what fifty real ones cost and deliver what neither does. So this roster separates the two ideas that tier table had welded together:

- **Authored vs. generated.** Everyone here is authored, and everyone here has a *deal* — which is the doc's own test for whether a character lands.
- **Named vs. built.** Only the eight Rivals are ever candidates for a full `ADCCCharacterBase`, and even those get built two or three at a time. The rest are dialogue lines, announcements, graffiti and leaderboard rows.

That is also how the source material behaves. The System AI reads names out constantly, the audience knows hundreds of crawlers, and a given party meets a handful. A world with 33 names in it and six bodies feels far larger than one with twelve of each.

**Nothing here duplicates an existing NPC.** The current cast is corporate monsters (Voldemortgage, the Debt Eaters, the Auditor, the Notary) and service NPCs (the bartender, the cook, the divers, the Tailings elders, the new bookie and snack vendor). There were no named rival *crawlers* at all before this.

### Writing rules used

- **The old job is the whole build.** `Docs/dungeon-tone-bible.md` rule 5, specific beats generic — a tow operator, an OSHA inspector and a sommelier are three completely different characters with no new art required, which matters because art is the constraint here and code is not.
- **The System never mocks a real death beat** (tone bible rule 4). It mocks the format around one. Several entries below are built so the joke lands on the show, not the person.
- **The volunteer/survivor distinction** from `story-premise.md` is stated plainly exactly once, in Omar Bellinger-Khan's entry, with no joke attached.
- No reuse of the books' actual characters. All original, written in their spirit.

## Rivals — promote to a full `ADCCCharacterBase` (8)

The only ones that ever become real actors with stats, and even then not all at once. `Docs/population-design.md` budgets around twelve full characters for the entire game, so **do not build all eight.** Pick the two or three the party keeps colliding with and leave the rest as names until they earn it.

### Renata Vasquez-Pham — *“The Closer”*

<sub>she/her · 51 · Luxury realtor, Cherry Creek · Alive · floors 1-3</sub>

**The deal:** She sells other crawlers things that are not hers, and the paperwork always holds up.

Twenty-two years moving property nobody needed at prices nobody questioned. The Collapse changed her inventory and nothing else. She has sold the same safe room to three separate parties and been paid by all three. The System adores her, because contracts are its native language and she speaks it without an accent - it has quietly begun honouring her deeds as though she had the authority to write them. Nobody has established yet whether that means she does, or whether the System is simply enjoying the bit.

**Hook:** Vendor selling map access, safe-room rights and 'cleared' passage. Some of it is real. Working out which is a Charisma contest, not a hidden roll.  
**Sponsor:** Front Range Title & Escrow

### Otis Lange — *“Overtime”*

<sub>he/him · 63 · RTD driver, Route 15 Colfax · Alive · floors 2+</sub>

**The deal:** He is still driving the route, and the route now goes down.

Thirty-one years up and down Colfax. He came down in uniform and has not taken it off. Somewhere on Floor 2 he found something with wheels and started running a schedule, and he keeps it: he picks up, he does not exceed the limit, he does not deviate, and he does not stop for anything trying to kill you. He is not brave. He is on the clock, and the clock is the last piece of the old world still working.

**Hook:** A moving safe zone on a fixed timetable. Miss the stop and wait for the next one. Never fights, cannot be hurried, will not be argued with.  
**Sponsor:** (declines - still, somehow, union)

### Brick Halloran — *“Mile High's Own”*

<sub>he/him · 29 · Broncos practice squad, never activated · Alive · floors 1-4</sub>

**The deal:** The most sponsored crawler on the floor is a genuinely kind man being fattened for a ratings beat.

Two seasons carrying a blocking sled for a team that never played him, beloved by exactly one bar in Englewood. The System found him in week one and gave him everything - gear drops, soft spawns, a highlight package with music over it. He shares the gear. He calls out traps for strangers. He has no idea he is a story arc, and arcs end. Killing him yourself would pay an obscene amount of Favor and would be the single most hated thing this party could do.

**Hook:** Joinable ally who buffs the party and is scheduled to die. The schedule may be breakable. The moral trap is the content.  
**Sponsor:** LIQUIDATOR Energy (three-floor exclusivity)

### Yuki Ordonez-Frame — *“Appetite”*

<sub>she/her · 34 · Competitive eater, taqueria on Federal · Alive · floors 3+</sub>

**The deal:** She eats the dungeon, and the dungeon has started eating back.

Forty-eight tacos, regional record, laminated certificate behind the register. On Floor 1 she worked out that consuming monster parts lends you their traits for a while. She is four floors down now, formidable, and no longer entirely the shape she arrived in. She is warm, chatty and completely without malice. She would like to know what your party tastes like. Purely professionally.

**Hook:** Rival who is stronger every time you meet her, always wearing an ability the party has already seen on something they killed.  
**Sponsor:** Rocky Mountain Rendering Co.

### (withholds his name) — *“The Sommelier”*

<sub>he/him · 47 · Floor sommelier, Larimer Square · Alive · floors 2-5</sub>

**The deal:** He finds a monster's weakness by tasting its blood, and he will not shut up about the finish.

Twenty years on the floor of a restaurant that has not existed for some time. He refuses to give his name to anyone, on the grounds that a name is a liability inside a broadcast product - which is the most genre-aware sentence anyone in Denver has said out loud. He is right. The System cannot reference him in an announcement, cannot sponsor him and cannot build him into a story. His Favor is catastrophic and he considers this the point.

**Hook:** Sells real boss resistances. His namelessness is a live mechanic: announcements skip him, which is a genuine tactical advantage the party can borrow.  
**Sponsor:** (unsponsorable - no name on file)

### Dolores 'Dee' Okonkwo-Reyes — *“The Appraiser”*

<sub>she/her · 58 · Estate liquidator · Alive · floors 1+</sub>

**The deal:** She follows parties at a polite distance and prices what they leave behind.

Thirty years of walking into houses after somebody died and putting a number on what was left. The job did not change. She never starts anything, is unfailingly courteous, and is always about eleven metres behind you. If you clear a room she prices the room. If you die in it she prices you, respectfully, and the audience finds this unbearably funny.

**Hook:** Non-hostile loot competitor. Can be raced, negotiated with or robbed - and robbing her costs Favor, because she is far more popular than the party.  
**Sponsor:** Estate Solutions of the Front Range

### Augustine Pell — *“Compliance”*

<sub>they/them · 41 · OSHA inspector, Region VIII · Alive · floors 2-6</sub>

**The deal:** They are citing the dungeon for safety violations, and the citations are landing.

Career federal inspector, with a clipboard that survived the Collapse for reasons nobody can explain. They have filed 611 violations against Dungeon Crawler World: Earth - inadequate egress, unguarded machinery, no fall protection on the Falls. The System is obliged to log every one. It has started, very quietly, fixing some of them, and a fixed hazard is a hazard your party no longer has to survive.

**Hook:** Reporting a hazard to them permanently disables that trap for everyone. The System resents this and adjusts the difficulty somewhere the party is not looking.  
**Sponsor:** (offer pending - they have not signed the release)

### Marisol Trejo-Adeyemi — *“Second Opinion”*

<sub>she/her · 38 · Trauma nurse, Denver Health · Alive · floors 1-3</sub>

**The deal:** She will save anyone, including the thing that just tried to kill you.

Eleven years in a trauma bay where you do not get to pick. She has not adjusted that policy and does not intend to. She has stabilised crawlers, stabilised a Debt Eater, and once spent an hour working on a Survey Drone before establishing it had never been alive. Parties love her right up until the first time she saves someone they wanted dead.

**Hook:** Healer who will absolutely revive an enemy in front of you. Recruitable, at the price of never getting to choose who lives.  
**Sponsor:** Cherry Creek Urgent Care (regional)

## Encounter crawlers — one scripted meeting each (9)

Real enough to talk to, thin enough to be an `ADCCDialogueNPC` with a mesh and a few lines. None of them need a stats component. Each exists to hand the party exactly one thing — a repair, a timing window, a route, a moral problem — and then stop.

### Sister Agnetha Roule — *“The Complication”*

<sub>she/her · 71 · Hospice chaplain · Alive · floors 1-2</sub>

**The deal:** She keeps performing last rites, and it is corrupting the death metrics.

Forty years of sitting with people at the end, and she does the identical job here. The problem is upstream: a death with a witness who calls it sacred does not convert cleanly into whatever the show is actually harvesting, and somebody several layers above the System AI has begun to notice a shortfall in the Denver numbers.

**Hook:** Meeting her enters the party in a ledger they cannot see. She will bless a body for free, and the party's next death behaves differently.  
**Sponsor:** (none)

### Cormac Bui — *“Merge”*

<sub>he/him · 44 · Tow operator, I-25 corridor · Alive · floors Traffic floors</sub>

**The deal:** He towed wrecks off the freeway for twenty years and now tows things that are still moving.

Found a rig on the gridlock floor and put it straight back to work. He will haul your dead vehicle, your unconscious friend, or a monster you would rather fight somewhere with better footing. He invoices for all three. The invoices are real, itemised, and the System enforces them with enthusiasm.

**Hook:** Vehicle recovery, plus the tactical option of dragging a boss into terrain that suits you. Unpaid invoices become a debt encounter later.  
**Sponsor:** Front Range Recovery & Salvage

### Nadia Ferreira-Stokes — *“Hail Damage”*

<sub>she/her · 36 · Body shop owner, Aurora · Alive · floors 2-4</sub>

**The deal:** She repairs armour with a dent puller and absolutely no ceremony.

Built a business on Colorado's only real natural predator, the summer hailstorm, and she has never once been impressed by damage. She will fix your breastplate in four minutes, tell you what you did wrong to earn the dent, and quote you for the paint separately. She considers the monsters a supply-side problem.

**Hook:** Field repair. Restores durability cheaply and identifies what kind of attack made each mark - free intel on what is hunting the party.  
**Sponsor:** Casa de Hail Auto Body

### Terrance Vo-Bell — *“Encore”*

<sub>he/him · 26 · Sound engineer, Red Rocks · Alive · floors Red Rock</sub>

**The deal:** He knows exactly where every sound in the amphitheatre goes, and so should you.

Six summers mixing front-of-house on the best natural stage on the continent. He came down with in-ear monitors still around his neck and has been mapping the acoustics of every arena since. He can tell you where a boss cannot hear you, which is a thing bosses do not know about themselves. He is having, by some distance, the best time of anyone in this document.

**Hook:** Marks acoustic dead zones in arena fights - real stealth geometry on a floor that otherwise has none.  
**Sponsor:** Mile High Audio

### Holly Drescher-Kaur — *“The Understudy”*

<sub>she/her · 23 · Cliff diver, Cliff Diver Cantina · Alive · floors 1</sub>

**The deal:** She survived the dive nobody survives, and management considers this a scheduling problem.

Nineteen when she started diving into a chlorinated pool for tourists eating bad enchiladas, twenty-three when the venue was repossessed with the staff still inside. She is the only diver who came back up. The Cantina's new management has her listed for tonight's show, and every night's show, in perpetuity, and the listing is the thing keeping her there.

**Hook:** Floor 1 rescue. Freeing her means voiding a staff contract - a puzzle, not a fight. She joins as a mobility specialist who reaches places the party cannot.  
**Sponsor:** Cliff Diver Cantina (staff, non-negotiable)

### Elias Winterhalder — *“Local Time”*

<sub>he/him · 68 · Broadcast meteorologist, 40 years · Alive · floors Any</sub>

**The deal:** He is still doing the forecast, and down here the forecast is accurate.

Four decades of pointing at a green wall and being wrong about Saturday with tremendous warmth. Underground, with no weather left to get wrong, he has quietly become the most reliable information source in Denver: he tracks hazard cycles, spawn timings and pressure changes, and delivers them in the exact cadence of a man telling you to take a jacket.

**Hook:** Gives real timing windows for environmental hazards. The forecast is genuinely correct, which the party will not believe until it saves them.  
**Sponsor:** Channel 4 (defunct; he still says it)

### Priya Benavidez-Okafor — *“Chain of Custody”*

<sub>she/her · 33 · Evidence technician, DPD · Alive · floors 2-5</sub>

**The deal:** She is documenting everything the dungeon does, for a trial that cannot happen.

Bagged and tagged for nine years and cannot stop. She photographs kill sites, logs serial numbers on System-issued gear, and maintains a chain of custody on evidence for a prosecution with no court, no jurisdiction and no defendant that can be served. The System finds this hilarious. It has started leaving things out for her.

**Hook:** Trades from her archive. Her logs are the party's only route to knowing what the show did to a previous party in the room they are standing in.  
**Sponsor:** (refuses, on evidentiary grounds)

### Ruben Astorga-Mbeki — *“The Landlord”*

<sub>he/him · 55 · Slumlord, four buildings on Federal · Alive · floors 1-3</sub>

**The deal:** He was foreclosing on people before the Syndicate made it a format, and he wants credit for it.

Ran four buildings badly and profitably for twenty years, and arrived here genuinely excited - he recognised the business model immediately and assumed there would be a job in it. There was not. The System does not need middle management. He has spent three floors trying to get a meeting and has begun, slowly, to work out which side of the transaction he is on.

**Hook:** Comic antagonist who becomes a tragedy on a delay. Sells information he does not realise is worthless, and later, information he does not realise is priceless.  
**Sponsor:** (applications: 14 - responses: 0)

### June Abernathy-Cruz — *“Show of Hands”*

<sub>they/them · 30 · ASL interpreter, city contracts · Alive · floors 2-4</sub>

**The deal:** They can talk to the things that were never given a voice module.

Interpreted for city council, hospitals and one very long murder trial. Down here they worked out that several monster types signal intent with their bodies in a way that is consistent, learnable, and completely ignored by everyone busy screaming. They have now held four conversations with things the party has only ever fought.

**Hook:** Unlocks a non-combat resolution with at least one enemy family. The System does not offer this option and is visibly annoyed that it exists.  
**Sponsor:** (under review - 'accessibility' is a difficult sell to the audience)

## Rumour crawlers — named, never rendered (8)

**These cost nothing to build and carry most of the world.** They are names in bar dialogue, in System announcements, and in other crawlers' warnings. This is how DCC itself works: the show reads names out constantly and you meet almost none of them. Any one can be promoted to Encounter or Rival later without rewriting a word — that is the point of holding them here.

### 'The Quiet Floor' (unidentified)

<sub>Unknown · floors unknown</sub>

**The deal:** Somebody is clearing rooms ahead of the party and leaving nothing behind to show for it.

Four separate parties have walked into an encounter already finished. No bodies, no loot, no damage to the room, and no announcement - and the last one is what frightens people, because the System announces everything. The prevailing theory in the bar is that they are very good. The less popular theory is that they are not a crawler.

**Hook:** Long-running mystery seed. Can stay unresolved for floors. Pays off whenever it is useful and costs nothing until then.  
**Sponsor:** (n/a)

### August Kessler-Boone — *“Six Figures”*

<sub>he/him · 48 · Wealth manager · Alive · floors 4+</sub>

**The deal:** He is buying other crawlers' Favor, and somebody is selling.

Nobody in Denver has met him. Everybody in Denver has heard the terms: real gold, up front, for a permanent share of your audience attention, System-witnessed and irreversible. Several parties are known to have signed. Nobody knows what he is doing with the accumulated Favor, and nobody has thought carefully about what a man with no audience of his own would want it for.

**Hook:** Offstage economic threat. His buyers can appear as NPCs long before he does. A late escalation, not an early one.  
**Sponsor:** (owns three)

### Ada 'Mama' Kettleworth — *“The Kitchen”*

<sub>she/her · 66 · Church basement cook, Five Points · Alive · floors 3</sub>

**The deal:** There is a room three floors down where nobody is allowed to fight, and she enforces it.

Fed a congregation on Sunday and anyone who turned up the rest of the week, for thirty-one years. She has apparently done the same thing here, in a room the System has not managed to monetise. The rule is the rule: you eat, you leave your weapons at the door, and you do not bring it inside. Two parties who tested this are said to have left considerably worse off than the people they came for.

**Hook:** Neutral ground the party badly needs and cannot exploit. Whatever enforces her rule should never be shown directly.  
**Sponsor:** (none, and the System has stopped asking)

### Damien Ortiz-Halliwell — *“First Descent”*

<sub>he/him · 39 · Alpinist and cave diver · Unknown · floors unknown</sub>

**The deal:** He went down on day one, alone, and has never been announced dead.

Summited things without ropes and dived systems nobody had mapped, and walked into the Denver entry point eleven hours after the Collapse with a headlamp and no plan at all. The System announces every death. It has not announced his. Depending on who is telling it, that means he is the deepest living human being on Earth, or it means he found the thing at the bottom and it has better uses for him.

**Hook:** The party's eventual proof that the floors do not end. Best kept as a name on a wall, scratched deeper than anyone should have been able to reach.  
**Sponsor:** (none - predates the sponsorship layer)

### 'The Gardener' (unidentified)

<sub>Alive · floors 2-5</sub>

**The deal:** Somebody is planting things in the dungeon, and some of them are coming up.

Pockets of growing matter keep appearing in rooms with no light and no soil - arranged, deliberate, and in at least two cases edible. The System has classified them as an unlicensed environmental modification and is charging a fee it has nobody to bill. Whoever is doing it moves ahead of the parties and has never been seen.

**Hook:** Ambient hope. Small free heals in unexpected places. Reveal only if it is worth more revealed than not, which it probably is not.  
**Sponsor:** (n/a)

### Colette Nwachukwu-Barr — *“The Sponsor Killer”*

<sub>she/her · 42 · Class-action attorney · Alive · floors 3-6</sub>

**The deal:** She only kills crawlers who have signed sponsorship deals, and she has never once been wrong about who has.

Spent a career suing companies for what they put inside people. Down here she has decided that the sponsorship contract is the actual crime and the signatories are the actual accomplices, which is either a coherent moral position or six floors of rationalisation. Her Favor is enormous. The audience adores a heel with a thesis.

**Hook:** A direct threat the moment the party takes their first sponsor. Turns accepting a deal into a real decision instead of free money.  
**Sponsor:** (kills for it; will not take one)

### 'The 4:15' (unidentified)

<sub>Unknown · floors Any</sub>

**The deal:** Something arrives in the same room every day at 4:15 AM local time, and local time is a thing the System keeps mentioning.

The floor-open announcement always gives the local time, and it is always wrong except once a day. Crawlers who have been down long enough have noticed that at 4:15 AM, whatever room they happen to be in, something is briefly present and then is not. Nobody has been hurt by it. That is somehow worse.

**Hook:** A recurring timed beat that costs nothing to run. Never explain it. The moment it becomes a mechanic it stops being the best thing in the bar.  
**Sponsor:** (n/a)

### Wendell Amagi-Soto — *“The Cartographer”*

<sub>he/him · 59 · USGS surveyor · Deceased · floors 2</sub>

**The deal:** He mapped two entire floors correctly and the map is still down there somewhere.

Thirty years surveying the Front Range, and he applied every bit of it: proper triangulation, elevation profiles, a legend. He finished Floor 2 and was most of the way through Floor 3 when a Debt Eater found him. The map is real, it is accurate, and it is in a bag in a room nobody has identified. Four parties are actively looking.

**Hook:** A treasure hunt whose payoff is information, not gear. Whoever finds it gets the floor layout - worth more than any weapon, and far easier to author.  
**Sponsor:** (none)

## The Deceased — names on the leaderboard, the wall and the announcements (8)

Already dead when the party arrives. They exist as graffiti, achievement names, leaderboard rows and things NPCs mention. They are also the answer to the attrition problem: `UDCCPopulationSubsystem` reports hundreds of thousands of deaths as a number, and a number is wallpaper. One name attached to it is not.

### Grace Oyelaran-Pike — *“Ninety Seconds”*

<sub>she/her · 27 · Line cook · Deceased · floors 1</sub>

**The deal:** Held a doorway for ninety seconds so eleven strangers could get through it.

Nobody in that stairwell knew her name; they had it off the death announcement, which the System delivered in the same tone it uses for patch notes. Eleven people are alive on Floor 2 because of ninety seconds of work by someone who was not strong, was not armed, and was nobody's idea of a hero. Her name is now scratched into the wall of that stairwell fourteen separate times, in fourteen different hands.

**Hook:** The graffiti is the content. Put her name on that wall and let players find it without a quest marker.  
**Sponsor:** (none)

### Tobias Reinholt-Cruz — *“Statistically Speaking”*

<sub>he/him · 45 · Actuary · Deceased · floors 1</sub>

**The deal:** Calculated the survival odds correctly, announced them, and was killed by the resulting stampede.

He was right. That is the whole joke, and the System has never let it go - his numbers were accurate to within two percent, and the panic they caused killed forty people including him. The achievement named after him is awarded to any crawler who shares genuinely bad news with a group.

**Hook:** An achievement with a person behind it. Ties the existing achievement system to a name instead of a pun.  
**Sponsor:** (posthumous, briefly)

### Ingrid Solberg-Adeyemi — *“The Record”*

<sub>she/her · 31 · Ultramarathon runner · Deceased · floors 2</sub>

**The deal:** Held the fastest floor clear in Denver for six days and died eleven metres from the exit.

Ran a hundred miles at a time for fun, and treated the dungeon as terrain, which worked extremely well right up until it did not. Her time still tops the Denver leaderboard. The System has left it there, because an unbeaten record set by a dead woman is better engagement than a living champion, and it says so openly.

**Hook:** Her time is the number on the leaderboard the party is chasing. Beating it should feel bad.  
**Sponsor:** Continental Divide Nutrition

### Marcus Kettleby-Osei — *“Two Floors”*

<sub>he/him · 52 · Middle school principal · Deceased · floors 2</sub>

**The deal:** Got sixty-one children down two floors and did not get down the third.

Every account agrees on the count and none of them agree on how. He was not a fighter and had no skills worth listing. What he had was thirty years of moving large numbers of frightened young people through a building calmly, and it turns out that is a skill the dungeon had no answer for, until it simply outlasted him.

**Hook:** The reason there are children in the Floor 3 safe rooms. Do not show the death; show the sixty-one.  
**Sponsor:** (none)

### Lena Vasquez-Holt — *“Unsponsored”*

<sub>she/her · 19 · Student · Deceased · floors 1</sub>

**The deal:** Turned down four sponsorship offers, and the fifth thing that found her was not an offer.

Nineteen, first year, and stubborn in the specific way that only works if you live through it. She refused every deal on the grounds that she had not agreed to be in a show and was not going to start now, which the audience found charming and then irritating. Her Favor bottomed out. Favor is what the System spends on soft spawns.

**Hook:** The cautionary tale the bar tells about refusing sponsorship - deliberately in tension with Colette Nwachukwu-Barr, who kills people for accepting.  
**Sponsor:** (refused: 4)

### Harold Mbeki-Strand — *“The Regular”*

<sub>he/him · 74 · Retired; drank in the same bar since 1979 · Deceased · floors 1</sub>

**The deal:** Died in his usual seat, in a bar that had been dragged underground with him still in it.

The Collapse took every roofed structure on Earth in the same instant, and a great many people were indoors. Harold was on his stool. Whatever the dungeon did to that building, it kept the bar and it kept him, and he stayed there eleven days being served by staff who were also not okay, and then he stopped. Nobody moved him for a while. It did not seem right.

**Hook:** Sets the Bar's tone in one beat: the staff are not fine, and the regulars were here first.  
**Sponsor:** (none)

### Samira Doyle-Fontaine — *“Patch 1.4.2”*

<sub>she/her · 35 · QA lead, games industry · Deceased · floors 3</sub>

**The deal:** Found an exploit that trivialised a boss, and was patched out along with it.

Twelve years of breaking software professionally, and she broke the Voldemortgage encounter inside forty minutes - a genuine, reproducible sequence break. She wrote it up. She shared it. Four parties used it. Then came a balance patch, delivered with the System's usual warmth, and the patch notes listed her by name under 'Fixed'.

**Hook:** The party can still find her writeup. Any exploit they discover now carries the implication that using it puts them in the next patch notes.  
**Sponsor:** (revoked at time of patch)

### Omar Bellinger-Khan — *“Volunteer”*

<sub>he/him · 22 · Warehouse picker, DIA freight · Deceased · floors 1</sub>

**The deal:** Signed up on day two because the alternative was the surface, and lasted nine hours.

The four million are volunteers, which is the distinction the System leans on whenever anyone objects to any of this. Omar volunteered. He had seen what was left up there and did the arithmetic in about a minute, and the arithmetic was correct - the show is the better option, which is the actual horror and not the monsters. Nine hours. The announcement gave his name, his sponsor status, and the local time.

**Hook:** Use his name in the intake announcement. He is what 'volunteer' means, said plainly, exactly once, with no joke attached.  
**Sponsor:** (none - never reached the offer stage)

## Using this

1. **Free, no build.** Add the Rumour and Deceased names to `DT_DCCDialogue` as bar gossip, and to the System's announcement lines. That alone makes Denver feel populated, and it needs no C++ and no editor restart.
2. **Cheap.** The eight Encounter crawlers are `ADCCDialogueNPC` placements with a mesh, a handful of lines and one thing to give. Still no new C++.
3. **Only when earned.** Promote a Rival to a real character class. Dolores Okonkwo-Reyes is the natural first — `population-design.md` already nominated “The Appraiser” as the first promotion, because *“follows parties at a polite distance and takes what they leave behind”* is already a mechanic. This roster just gives them a person.

`Docs/DT_DCCCrawlers.csv` sits beside this file, ready to import once an `FDCCCrawlerDef` row struct exists. That struct is a reflection change and therefore a full build cycle, so it is deliberately **not** done yet — the roster is useful as prose and dialogue first, and the table should earn itself by being used.
